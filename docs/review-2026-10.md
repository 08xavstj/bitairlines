# Review, October 2026: economy, what to fix, and the competition

## 1. Why the game felt like it loses money

A Python port of the daily loop (demand, waiting passengers, awareness, seasons, polar daylight, weather, crew limits, wear,
salaries, head office) shows that sensible play earns: one Caravan on Inuvik to Tuktoyaktuk once a day makes about $356k a
year, Inuvik to Sachs Harbour about $631k. The losses come from traps:

| Cause | Size |
|---|---|
| Extra flights on a thin route carry nobody new; each extra Caravan round trip costs about $950 a day | Caravan YEV-YUB: +$356k a year at 1 a day, -$505k at 6 a day |
| The forecast ranks aircraft by payback only and prices maintenance as a 12-year-old aircraft, so it pushes the DC-3 | Real DC-3 upkeep about 55% higher than shown; flown daily on YEV-YSY it loses $670k a year |
| A new route starts at half demand and the game starts in the polar night | A January day on YEV-YUB: -$29 instead of the forecast +$1,234 |
| Head office grew with the square of the level | Level 3 cost $394k a year, more than a whole Caravan earns |
| Bases cost more than they save for a small fleet | A hangar: $675k plus $225 a day |
| Pilot salaries were added on top of a crew cost that already paid them | Twin Otter: $263 a day, 81% of its profit on YEV-YSY |
| The Money screen counts investments (bases, fuel bought ahead, slots) as losses, and pilot pay lands as one red bar a month | Looks worse than it is |
| Mail jobs lose money once the empty flights out and back are counted | Average -$81, a loss half the time |

Changed in this round (rules version 3): crew cost per block hour 110 to 80, head office `120 x L(L+1)/2`
(level 3: $1,080 to $720 a day), new routes start at 65% of demand and learn twice as fast, hangar upkeep 150 to 60 and fuel
depot 60 to 30 a day, mail jobs pay 2.2 times the going rate.

Still to do (screen changes, the bigger win):
- Route card: show seat load and warn when a schedule flies empty seats ("each extra flight a day costs about $950 and carries
  nobody new"). Red when the route loses money.
- Forecast: price wear at the aircraft's real age, show the slow first months, rank aircraft by profit a day among those that
  pay back within 4 years.
- Money: split "flying" from "investments"; book pilot pay daily.
- A quiet notice when a route lost money last month.
- Job board: show the expected profit after the empty flights, not only the pay.
- Base sheet: show daily upkeep next to the expected saving.

## 2. Fix, change, remove

### Must fix before release
1. Save when the app goes to the background. **Done.**
2. Privacy manifest. **Done.**
3. "Declare bankruptcy" ends the game with one tap and sits under "Emergency loan". Add a confirm step.
4. Weather issues stay in the Inbox after the weather clears. Remove them when the closure ends.
5. A float-only aircraft can be bought by an airline whose home has no water, and is stranded. Refuse or hide it.
6. Real trademarks: aircraft makers and models, the "AA" example code, "the Flying Doctor". Rename or license (see app-store.md).
7. The map redraws terrain, every airport and every label about 15 times a second. Cache the terrain per camera position,
   index airports by area, cache label layout. Battery and heat on older phones. (Thinning the map to 2,427 airports helps.)

### Should change
- Two difficulty pickers in the new-airline flow (Rules and Easy/Standard/Hard) look like the same choice. Merge them.
- Settings cannot be reached in a game; the only menu is the hidden airline-code button. Add a visible menu.
- The side rail has 9 tabs and overflows when the guide is showing.
- Map labels overlap and get cut at the screen edge; measure text width and skip labels under the toolbar.
- Truncated names ("DHC-2 BEAVER FLOA.", "SACHS."), "Waiting until 06:00" without the day.
- The red "Close" on a route card reads like closing the panel; call it "End route".
- Limit the airline name to about 20 plain letters (the pixel font has no accents).
- A bankrupt save stays on the Continue list.
- Route forecasts are recomputed on every clock tick; cache them per route.

### Consider removing or simplifying for 1.0
- Hide until later levels: slots, rival rankings, hubs.
- Fuel bought ahead: keep only the drifting price.
- Service level per route: fold into the fare.
- Pilots: keep automatic hiring and a salary line; drop manual hiring and type courses.
- Merge Bases into the airport panel on the map, Jobs into the map or Inbox, Kits into the aircraft sheet. The rail drops to
  about six tabs: Map, Fleet, Routes, Hangar, Money, Inbox.
- Modes: Normal and Sandbox, with Realism as a switch.

## 3. The competition

| Game | Loop | Money | What players say |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pocket Planes (NimbleBit) | Load and fly each flight by hand, unlock regions | Free, IAP, ad for premium currency | Charming; hand-routing gets tedious; lost saves; abandoned |
| Pocket Trains | Same, trains | Crates for special trains | Better freemium design than Planes |
| Airline Manager 4 | Routes, fuel and CO2 buying, alliances | Free, cash packs $0.99 to $24.99 | Easy and Realism modes praised; fuel prices punish late starters |
| Airlines Manager: Plane Tycoon | Routes, R&D, Pro and Tycoon modes | Free, IAP and many ads | Ads and pay-to-win feel are the top complaints |
| AirlineSim | Deep schedules, persistent worlds | Pay per day per world | Hardcore, respected for depth |
| Jumbo Airport Story (Kairosoft) | Pixel airport building | Premium $5.99 | 4.8 stars, no ads; repetitive late game |
| Idle Airport Tycoon | Idle upgrades, money while away | Free, rewarded ad doubles offline money | Praised for few ads |
| Airline Executive (new) | One plane to an empire over 70 years | Says "no ads, no premium currency, no pay-to-win" | Closest new rival in positioning |

### Worth adding, best value for effort first
1. A weekly goal ("deliver 2 t of freight to Kugluktuk this week"), seeded by date so it needs no server. Pocket Planes'
   best-loved hook; scores on a Game Center leaderboard.
2. "While you were away" on return: what the fleet earned. The natural place for an optional ad.
3. Rare finds: a special livery or a rare used aircraft that turns up through jobs.
4. Aviation history: start in a chosen decade, with aircraft arriving over time.
5. Clear profit per route everywhere (players of every rival complain about confusing money).

### Avoid
Forced ads and long ad loops, pay-to-win, a premium currency, energy timers, lost saves (iCloud now), punishing late starters,
and going quiet after launch: plan small regular updates (a new region, new aircraft).

### Store page
- Name and subtitle carry search: for example "Bit Airlines: Pixel Tycoon" and "Fly bush planes, build routes".
- Keywords (100 characters, no repeats of the title): airline, manager, plane, pilot, aviation, arctic, idle, simulator,
  airport, cargo, retro, sim.
- Icon: one bold pixel aircraft on a flat colour, readable at small sizes.
- Screenshots: the first three matter most. 1) "Start a bush airline in the Arctic", 2) the map with real places and routes
  flying themselves, 3) the livery and logo editor, then jobs, the fleet, rivals.
- Say plainly in the promotional text: no forced ads, never pay-to-win. That is what the reviews ask for.

Sources for the competitor notes: TouchArcade and Pocket Gamer reviews of Pocket Planes, NimbleBit's save FAQ, Game
Developer and GamesBrief on Pocket Trains, the App Store, Steam and AppFollow pages of each game, GameRefinery on Kolibri's
ads, Unity and AppSamurai on rewarded ads, Apple's product page optimisation guide.
